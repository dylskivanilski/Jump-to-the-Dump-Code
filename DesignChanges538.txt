A Hop, Skip, and a Jump to the Dump!
Dylan Fermo - CS-583-03 - Professor Houston
Changes From the Design Document
=====================================================

CONTROLS (final)
- A/D: walk left/right (only while on the ground)
- Space: hold to charge a jump, release to jump (auto-jumps at full charge)
- A/D held while releasing Space: aims the jump left or right
- Esc: pause / resume
- Mouse: click menu buttons

-----------------------------------------------------
1. LEVEL STRUCTURE
Proposal: Unsure whether to make 1-3 levels.
Final: One continuous vertical level about 4 screens tall. The first section is a
beginner area with wide platforms and short gaps; the upper section has narrower
platforms and longer drops. Difficulty scales with height, as planned, without
needing multiple levels.

2. CHECKPOINTS
Proposal: Checkpoint flags (maybe).
Final: Since the game has no deaths or respawning (like Jump King), a normal
respawn checkpoint didn't fit. Instead, once the player clears the beginner
section, a "safety floor" closes underneath them with a bell sound. After that,
a bad fall can only drop the player to that floor, never all the way back to the
start. This keeps the lose-progress risk/reward from the proposal while keeping
the game fair.

3. OBSTACLES
Proposal: Obstacles (unspecified).
Final: Added a bouncy tire stack that launches the player upward when they land on
it. It can act as a shortcut if used well, or throw the player somewhere bad if
used carelessly.

4. JUMP MECHANIC DETAILS
Added several details that weren't in the proposal:
- No air control: once you jump, you're committed. This makes every jump a decision.
- The jump fires automatically at full charge, so the player can't hold forever.
- Wall bounce: hitting a wall mid-air bounces the player back off it.
- The player sprite squashes down while charging, like a spring, so the player can
  see the charge.

5. FALLING CONSEQUENCE
Proposal: Falling loses progress.
Final: Falling a long distance also briefly stuns the player and plays a "splat"
sound, so a big fall feels like a real setback.

6. UI ADDITIONS
- Charge meter that fills and changes from green to red as the jump charges.
- Height display showing current height and best height reached. Seeing your best
  height after a fall gives the player a reason to keep climbing.
- Victory screen showing total time and number of big falls, with Play Again and
  Main Menu buttons.
- Pause menu with Resume, Restart, and Main Menu buttons.

7. AUDIO
Proposal: Landing, falling, jumping, victory, background music (maybe), optional
environment sounds.
Final: Jump, land, big-fall splat, wall bump, tire bounce, checkpoint bell, pause,
and victory sounds. Sounds are played with slight random pitch variation so repeated
jumps don't sound robotic. Background music and ambient sounds were cut: the game is
about focus and precise timing, and the gameplay sounds give the important feedback.

8. ART
Final: Uses a junkyard tileset and worker character sprite. The dump at the top is
built from trash pile pieces, barrels, tires, and hazard signs instead of a single
goal object.

CUT FROM SCOPE
- Multiple levels
- Background music and ambient environment sounds
